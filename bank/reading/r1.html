阅读 Passage R1 · 城市为何种树

练习文本（ETS 风格，非官方真题）。训练目标：段落定位 + 因果/目的题。

For most of the twentieth century, city planners treated trees as decoration — a pleasant bonus to be added once the roads, pipes, and buildings were done. That view has shifted decisively. Today, urban forests are studied as infrastructure, as essential to a city's functioning as its sewer system. The change came from a pile of research showing that trees do quiet, measurable work that cash and concrete cannot easily replace.

The most obvious job is cooling. A mature tree shades pavement and releases water vapor through its leaves, dropping the temperature of the air around it by several degrees. On a hot afternoon, a street lined with maples can feel noticeably cooler than a bare one a block away. In cities where summer heat already sends people to hospitals, that difference is not cosmetic — it is public health.

Trees also manage water. Their roots and the soil around them soak up rainfall that would otherwise rush into storm drains, picking up oil and trash on the way. By slowing and filtering this flow, a single street of trees can spare a city millions in drainage upgrades. Planners in several flood-prone cities now plant deliberately, mapping where water collects and putting roots exactly there.

Perhaps least appreciated is the effect on people. Studies consistently find that residents of greener neighborhoods report lower stress and walk more. Whether the cause is cleaner air, quieter streets, or simply something pleasant to look at, the outcome shows up in health data. A city that plants trees, it turns out, is also a city that quietly treats its own population.

题目

Q1

题干：According to Paragraph 1, how did planners traditionally view city trees? 选项： A. As essential infrastructure like sewers B. As a decorative extra added after essentials C. As a source of public health data D. As a cheap replacement for concrete 答案：B 解析：第一段首句说，二十世纪的规划者把树当作 decoration（装饰），是道路、管道、建筑完成后的"pleasant bonus"，对应 B 的"装饰性附加物"。A 是今日的看法，非传统。

Q2

题干：The word "cosmetic" in Paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to 选项： A. scientific B. superficial C. expensive D. permanent 答案：B 解析：前文说降温差异"不是 cosmetic — 是 public health"，即不是表面好看，而是真实健康影响。cosmetic 本义"化妆的/表面的"，此处取" superficial（肤浅的、表面的）"。

Q3

题干：According to Paragraph 3, how do trees help with water? 选项： A. They pump water back into the pipes B. They soak up and filter rainfall, easing drains C. They prevent storms from reaching the city D. They replace expensive drainage systems entirely 答案：B 解析：第三段说树的根和土吸收降雨（soak up rainfall），减缓并过滤水流（slowing and filtering this flow），从而减轻排水系统压力，对应 B。A、C、D 均过度或曲解。

Q4

题干：Why do flood-prone cities now plant trees "deliberately" (Paragraph 3)? 选项： A. To make neighborhoods look uniform B. To place roots where water actually collects C. To reduce the number of storm drains D. To attract more residents to move in 答案：B 解析：第三段末句：这些城市"mapping where water collects and putting roots exactly there"（画出积水点、把根正好种在那），即按积水位置精准种植，对应 B。

Q5

题干：What is the main idea of the passage? 选项： A. Trees are cheaper to plant than to maintain B. Urban trees provide measurable, practical benefits beyond beauty C. Cities should replace sewers with urban forests D. Neighborhood stress is caused by a lack of trees 答案：B 解析：全文主线：树从"装饰"重新被认识为"基础设施"，并在降温、治水、居民健康三方面带来可量化实用收益，对应 B。A 未提；C 曲解（非替换）；D 把相关当因果。
